Who are the players, and what does each one actually do?
  • NASFA — the association that sponsors the plan. Its long standing as a bona fide association is what makes the arrangement legally possible. You cannot build a MEWA/AHP without one.
  • Agency Network — partner on the association side, bringing the member pool and handling communication to agents.
  • Brown & Brown (TJ) — our broker. Built and filed the MEWA, negotiates with the carrier, manages underwriting.
  • Blue Cross Blue Shield of Illinois — the carrier that insures the plan and pays the claims.
What is a MEWA / AHP in plain English?

It's a legal structure that lets many separate small employers — independent agency owners in the same profession — buy health coverage as one large group instead of as hundreds of tiny ones.

Large groups get large-group pricing, large-group underwriting rules, and plan designs that no single agency could access alone. That's the entire thesis: scale buys leverage.

The Plan Itself

Is the plan fully insured or self-funded?
Fully Insured

This was asked directly and answered directly. The plan is fully insured — it is not self-funded.

Then who carries the risk if claims run high?

Because the plan is fully insured, the carrier carries the claims risk — not NASFA, not Agency Network, and not you as an individual agency owner.

That was a deliberate design choice, and it's the answer to nearly every "what if it goes badly" question.

What plan designs will be offered?

We are working to mirror what you have today: two PPO plans and one HSA plan.

The goal is that this feels familiar, not foreign.

Is the plan ACA compliant?
Yes

And that has a direct consequence worth understanding: because the plan is ACA compliant, there are no medical questions and no pre-existing condition exclusions.

So there's no medical underwriting at all?
Correct — none

No medical underwriting. No pre-existing condition exclusions. Your health history — or a team member's — does not affect eligibility.

This was the single most-asked question going into the call, and the answer is clean.

Will dental and vision be available?
Yes — both

Dental and vision options will be provided through Blue Cross Blue Shield.

Who Is Eligible

What are the eligibility requirements, plainly?

Two hard requirements:

  1. You must be a NASFA member. Join at nasfa.com.
  2. You must have at least one full-time W-2 employee — 30+ hours per week. This is what legally qualifies you as an "employer group."
I'm a sole proprietor with no employees. Can I join?
Not currently eligible

If you have no full-time W-2 employee, you do not qualify as an employer group, and you are ineligible under the current structure.

We know this affects agents who are between hires or intentionally running lean, and we know it's disappointing. If you are actively hiring, fill the position and you're in.

Do I have to offer the plan to my whole team?
Yes

The plan must be offered to all full-time team members.

Do I have to pay for my team's coverage?
No

You must offer the plan — you are not required to subsidize the cost. Whether you contribute, and how much, remains your decision as the employer.

Can I offer it to part-time employees (under 30 hours)?
Being confirmed

Whether an agent may optionally extend the plan to employees working fewer than 30 hours is still being worked out, and the answer may differ by state. We'll publish it as soon as it's settled.

Can retired agents participate? What if I retire while on the plan?
Under legal review

Eligibility for retired agents, and for agents who leave the business, is genuinely undecided right now and sits with the attorneys.

This was one of the most-asked questions before and during the call, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a comfortable one: we don't know yet, and we won't guess. The moment legal comes back, it goes in this FAQ.

Why does my participation change my price?

Because this is a pooled arrangement — the size and demographic quality of the group is exactly what the carrier prices against.
